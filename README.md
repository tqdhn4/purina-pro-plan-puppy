## The lineup, and why the names keep changing

Purina has reorganized Pro Plan's puppy line more than once in the last few years. What you'll still see written on older forum posts as "Focus Puppy" or "SAVOR Shredded Blend" now shows up in stores as plain Pro Plan Puppy Chicken & Rice — Petco's own product page for the shredded blend notes it "used to be known as SAVOR Shredded Blend Chicken and Rice Formula." If you're following a five-year-old recommendation and can't find the exact name, that's usually why.

Independent reviews put the dry puppy range at around ten recipes, with ratings averaging near four stars, and the canned puppy range separate — a smaller, more uneven group. The practical split inside the dry line is:

- **By protein and sensitivity.** Chicken & Rice is the default. Salmon & Rice under the Sensitive Skin & Stomach banner is the chicken-free option.
- **By adult size.** Puppy, Large Breed Puppy, and small/toy breed puppy formulas exist as distinct products, not just different bag sizes.
- **By texture.** Kibble-only, or the shredded blend with tender pieces mixed through it.

That's the whole map. Everything else is marketing copy.

## Which formula actually fits your puppy

### The default: Puppy Chicken & Rice

If your puppy has no history of itching, ear infections, or loose stools, start here. It's the recipe most widely stocked, which means it's the one most likely to be available in the retailer you already use — and availability is what keeps cashback and subscription discounts useful.

You'll see two versions of the same idea: standard kibble, and the shredded blend with soft pieces mixed in. The shredded version is the one Business Insider's 2026 puppy food guide picked as its best dry pick, with the caveat that some owners note the kibble pieces run large for very small mouths. If you have a Yorkie puppy, that matters.

### The chicken-free route: Sensitive Skin & Stomach, Salmon & Rice

Purina's answer for puppies with itchy skin or a stomach that objects to chicken. Salmon is the first ingredient, and the formula is built without corn, wheat, or soy. Chewy lists it as chicken-free, and 24 lb is the standard large bag.

Two things worth knowing before you switch. First, this recipe is more expensive per pound than the chicken version — noticeably so, and the numbers are below. Second, if your puppy's issue is a true food allergy rather than general sensitivity, a vet-directed elimination trial or prescription diet is a different conversation than a retail bag, and the cashback math doesn't apply to prescription food anyway (more on that later).

### Large breed: for puppies headed past 50 lb at maturity

Large Breed Puppy Chicken & Rice is not the regular puppy food in a bigger bag. It's formulated for puppies that will weigh over 50 lb as adults, with controlled calcium and phosphorus ratios to keep skeletal growth from running ahead of the joints that have to carry it, plus L-lysine and a lower calorie density than the standard puppy formula. The logic: a Great Dane puppy that grows at maximum speed is not a healthier Great Dane puppy.

There's also a Large Breed Sensitive Skin & Stomach version with salmon, for big puppies that can't do chicken. Feeding notes from breeders who raise large breeds suggest keeping a puppy on large breed puppy food until somewhere around 12 to 15 months before transitioning to adult food. Your vet's read on your individual dog's growth beats any general timeline.

### Small and toy breed formulas

These exist for one practical reason: kibble size. Very small puppies struggle with large pieces, and very large puppies can inhale pieces that are too small. If you have a Chihuahua or a Maltese puppy, don't buy the standard bag and hope.

### Wet food as a topper

The canned Puppy Chicken & Rice Entrée Classic gets mixed reviews from independent raters — Dog Food Advisor scores that particular recipe around 2.5 stars, against 3.5 for the salmon Sensitive Skin & Stomach can. Used as a topper to get a picky puppy eating, or to transition onto kibble, it's fine. As the whole diet, it costs more than kibble for the same nutrition.

## What's in the bag — and the two things critics flag

Take the shredded blend Chicken & Rice as the representative recipe. Guaranteed analysis per the product label: **28% protein minimum, 18% fat minimum, 3% fiber maximum**, with DHA from fish oil for brain and vision development, calcium and phosphorus for bone growth, and a guaranteed count of live *Bacillus coagulans* — **600 million CFU per pound** — for digestive support.

The ingredient list after chicken and rice: poultry by-product meal, corn protein meal, beef fat, soybean meal, whole grain wheat, whole grain corn. If you're coming from a boutique brand, that list is the part you may not like. Two specific criticisms come up consistently in independent reviews:

- **By-product meal and corn** are flagged as lower-cost protein and energy sources rather than defects in themselves. By-product meal is a concentrated protein ingredient; corn is an inexpensive energy source. Neither is unsafe.
- **Menadione sodium bisulfite complex**, a synthetic vitamin K form, appears in the vitamin premix and is called out by reviewers as a controversial inclusion that isn't an AAFCO-required nutrient. It's the single most common criticism of the line among independent raters.

Weigh that against the other side of the ledger, which is real: Pro Plan is made by a manufacturer with on-staff veterinary nutritionists, uses AAFCO feeding trials rather than formulation alone for its puppy profiles, and sits comfortably inside the WSAVA-style quality-control framework that veterinary nutritionists tend to favor. Customer ratings on the major retailer product pages for the main puppy recipes cluster in the **4.3 to 4.7 out of 5** range. This is a well-tested mainstream dog food, not a premium boutique one, and it's priced accordingly. One independent review site lands its dry puppy range at about four stars overall.

## Questions that come up constantly

**Is Pro Plan Puppy a good food?** For a mainstream, feeding-trial-tested puppy food at a mainstream price, yes. It's not a boutique recipe and it contains by-product meal, corn, and menadione — the menadione is the fair criticism. If those ingredients are dealbreakers for you, you'll pay more elsewhere for a food that isn't necessarily better tested.

**Puppy or Large Breed Puppy for my dog?** Decide on expected adult weight, not current weight. A puppy that will finish over 50 lb should be on a large breed formula for the calcium and phosphorus control and the lower calorie density. If your puppy will land under that, the standard bag is fine.

**Chewy or Amazon?** Base prices are the same on the 34 lb Chicken & Rice bag — $74.98 both places. Chewy's Autoship first-delivery discount is bigger than Amazon's Subscribe & Save, and the ongoing 5% Autoship matches or beats Amazon's tiered 5%, so the gap comes down to which layer of cashback is live and how much of your order qualifies. Check both before you commit to a subscription.

**Does cashback work on prescription Pro Plan Veterinary Diets?** No. Prescription food and pharmacy orders are excluded at the merchant level — applying the link won't change that. Your vet, or a pharmacy that prices prescriptions below vet clinic retail, is the better path there.

**Does cashback work on Autoship orders?** The platform's guidance is that eligible Chewy orders earn cashback on first and subsequent Autoship deliveries. Verify on the merchant page the day you order, because terms get updated.

**How long does a puppy stay on puppy food?** Most dogs transition to adult food somewhere around their first birthday; large breeds often later, sometimes 12 to 15 months. Body condition and growth rate matter more than the calendar, and that's a vet call.
